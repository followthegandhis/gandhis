Where the Money Moved
IT Was the Only Sector in Green (+2.9%) While Everything Else Bled Up to -2.4%
The market opened 1% higher on optimism around a potential India-US trade deal. Then Brent crude surged past $111/barrel intraday, and the rally evaporated. Sensex fell ~1,300 points from the day's high.
IT▲ 2.88%
PSU Bank▼ 0.15%

Educational Context

When the market opens strong and reverses hard, it's called a bull trap. The opening gap attracted buyers, then crude-driven selling overwhelmed them. Only one sector surviving in green signals defensive rotation — capital hiding in IT for dollar-revenue safety.

Wipro +3.4%, Infosys +3.0%, Tech Mahindra +2.7% — While Bajaj Finance Fell -3.5% and Eternal -3.6%
The top 3 Nifty gainers were all IT stocks. The top 3 losers were all consumption/financial names. Two completely different stories in the same index.

What the Data Shows

IT stocks benefit from a weaker rupee — they earn in dollars. When crude rises, the rupee weakens, and IT becomes a natural hedge. Meanwhile, Bajaj Finance and Eternal are domestic consumption plays that get hurt by rising input costs and tighter liquidity.

Why Breadth Matters

The Flow
FIIs Sold ₹413 Cr — Their Lightest Selling Day This October. DIIs Bought Again.
FII selling slowed dramatically — from ₹5,285 Cr last session to just ₹413 Cr today. But they're still net sellers. DIIs continue to absorb every dip.

The Pattern

FIIs have sold in 15 of 16 sessions this October — nearly ₹49,000 Cr gone. But today's ₹413 Cr is the lightest outflow of the month. Is the selling exhaustion beginning? DIIs have been net buyers in every single session — absorbing almost rupee-for-rupee what FIIs are dumping.

The Context
Brent $111, Rupee ₹95/$, VIX at 4-Month High — The Three Numbers That Killed Today's Rally
The market opened strong. Then three macro forces converged to reverse it.

Why These 3 Numbers Matter

Crude past $111 = higher import bill = wider current account deficit. Rupee at ₹95/$ = FII dollar returns erode further, accelerating outflows. VIX at 15.70 (4-month high) = options market pricing in more turbulence ahead. When all three move against you on the same day, even trade-deal optimism can't hold.

What is Gandhis?

Gandhis is a daily post-market educational guide for the Indian stock market. Every trading day at 8 PM, we publish 5 cards — which sectors moved, what institutions did, and the macro picture.

We go through data from NSE, BSE, and official sources so you don't have to. Five cards. Ninety seconds. That's it.

The 5 Cards — Every Day

Where the Money Moved — which sectors saw capital flow in or out
The Signal — the one earnings result or stock move that stood out
The Data Point — market breadth, VIX, and numbers beneath the surface
The Flow — FII and DII activity, who's buying, who's selling
The Context — crude, rupee, US yields, and global forces

We don't predict the market. We don't recommend stocks. We don't sell tips. We present publicly available data so patterns become visible.

How We Think

We focus on sector rotation — money doesn't leave the market, it moves. Most people look at the Nifty and react. We look at where the money is actually going.
