Why Breadth Matters

When 69% of stocks fall but the index drops only 1.11%, it means index heavyweights are holding up while the broader market is crumbling. VIX spiking 8.4% confirms the fear is real. This kind of breadth deterioration often precedes further weakness.

The Flow
FIIs Sold ₹5,285 Cr. DIIs Bought ₹4,120 Cr. The Tug of War Continues.
FIIs have now sold in 14 of 15 October sessions. DIIs have bought in all 15. The numbers are staggering.

The Pattern

FIIs are pulling money out of India at a pace not seen since March 2020. But DIIs — mutual funds, insurance companies, pension funds — are absorbing almost every rupee. This FII-DII tug of war is what's keeping the market from a sharper fall.

The Context
Brent $108, Rupee ₹95/$, US 10Y at 5.1% — The Three Forces Driving FII Exits
Three macro numbers explain why FIIs keep selling Indian equities.

Why These 3 Numbers Matter

US 10Y at 5.1% = risk-free dollar returns are attractive, pulling capital away from emerging markets. Brent at $108 = India's import bill stays elevated, pressuring the current account. Rupee at ₹95/$ = FII dollar returns erode, making India less attractive. All three are working against Indian equities simultaneously.
