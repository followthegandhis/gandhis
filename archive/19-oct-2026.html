When only one sector is green and it's a defensive/value play like PSU Banks, it signals institutional money rotating into safety. The market isn't just falling — it's choosing where to hide.

Two banks. Both beat estimates. Completely opposite stock reactions. The market is telling you something about expectations.

What the Data Shows

PNB was priced for mediocrity — any positive surprise gets rewarded. Axis Bank was priced for perfection — even beating estimates wasn't enough. This is called "buy the rumour, sell the news" and it reveals where institutional expectations were already positioned.

Why Breadth Matters

When 69% of stocks fall but the index drops only 1.11%, it means index heavyweights are holding up while the broader market is crumbling. VIX spiking 8.4% confirms the fear is real. This kind of breadth deterioration often precedes further weakness.

4
The Flow
FIIs Sold ₹5,285 Cr. DIIs Bought ₹4,120 Cr. The Tug of War Continues.
FIIs have now sold in 14 of 15 October sessions. DIIs have bought in all 15. The numbers are staggering.
FII Cash (19 Oct)-₹5,285 Cr
DII Cash (19 Oct)+₹4,120 Cr
FII Oct Total-₹41,400 Cr
DII Oct Total+₹43,400 Cr
The Pattern

FIIs are pulling money out of India at a pace not seen since March 2020. But DIIs — mutual funds, insurance companies, pension funds — are absorbing almost every rupee. This FII-DII tug of war is what's keeping the market from a sharper fall.

5
The Context
Brent $108, Rupee ₹95/$, US 10Y at 5.1% — The Three Forces Driving FII Exits
Three macro numbers explain why FIIs keep selling Indian equities.
Brent Crude$108.20 (▼ 1.51%)
USD/INR₹95.02 (▲ 0.12%)
US 10Y Yield5.10% (▲ 0.18%)
Why These 3 Numbers Matter

US 10Y at 5.1% = risk-free dollar returns are attractive, pulling capital away from emerging markets. Brent at $108 = India's import bill stays elevated, pressuring the current account. Rupee at ₹95/$ = FII dollar returns erode, making India less attractive. All three are working against Indian equities simultaneously.

What is Gandhis?

Gandhis is a daily post-market educational guide for the Indian stock market. Every trading day at 8 PM, we publish 5 cards — which sectors moved, what institutions did, and the macro picture.

We go through data from NSE, BSE, and official sources so you don't have to. Five cards. Ninety seconds. That's it.

The 5 Cards — Every Day

Where the Money Moved — which sectors saw capital flow in or out
The Signal — the one earnings result or stock move that stood out
The Data Point — market breadth, VIX, and numbers beneath the surface
The Flow — FII and DII activity, who's buying, who's selling
The Context — crude, rupee, US yields, and global forces

We don't predict the market. We don't recommend stocks. We don't sell tips. We present publicly available data so patterns become visible.

How We Think

We focus on sector rotation — money doesn't leave the market, it moves. Most people look at the Nifty and react. We look at where the money is actually going.
