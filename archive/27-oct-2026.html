Educational Context

After 4 days of selling, a relief bounce is normal — short sellers cover positions and bargain hunters step in. Metal leading suggests global commodity sentiment improved slightly. But the bounce was modest (+0.15%) — this isn't a reversal, it's a pause in the downtrend. IT flipping from last week's hero to this week's laggard shows how fast sector rotation works.

The Signal
Small-Caps Crashed — Gauge Erased Year-to-Date Gains. Margin Calls Forced Liquidation. Retail Investors Hit Hardest.
The small-cap index suffered its steepest fall in over five months on Friday, erasing its year-to-date gain. Retail investors who bought shares with borrowed money faced margin calls, forcing liquidation. The bigger question: will this trigger a slowdown in mutual fund flows into small- and mid-cap funds?
Small-Cap IndexYTD gain erased
Margin CallsForced selling
Midcap+0.45% (recovery)
What the Data Shows

Small-caps account for a large share of margin-funded positions. When prices fall, brokers issue margin calls — forcing investors to sell at any price. This creates a vicious cycle: selling begets more selling. The recovery today (+0.45% midcap) suggests the worst of the forced selling may be over, but redemption risk in small-cap mutual funds remains the key risk to watch.

The Pattern

FIIs have sold in 14 of 16 trading days this October. But the DII absorption rate of 178% today is remarkable — for every ₹1 FIIs sold, DIIs bought ₹1.78. The FII net short position of 2.77 lakh contracts is massive — if crude eases further or the Fed signals a pause, a short-covering rally could be explosive. Watch the Fed decision on Wednesday.
